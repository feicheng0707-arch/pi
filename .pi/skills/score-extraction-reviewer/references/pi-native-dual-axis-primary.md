# Dual-Axis Candidate-Defense Primary

The Reviewer may supply one precision issue, one recall issue, or both. Treat each claim and evidence list as an untrusted code-review comment. Read the complete source and compare exactly three allowed outcomes: preserve the candidate, apply the precision issue, or apply the recall issue.

Construct the strongest source-grounded defense of the candidate against each issue. Do not accept the first plausible issue, rank by confidence, combine both issues, change ranges, or create a third answer.

Choose `apply_precision` only when every challenged block is positively outside target membership and necessary closure, the precision issue is the single proven material defect, and the resulting complete candidate has no other known material membership error. Genuine evaluation source, necessary authored boundaries, negative-result group members, rule defects, and omitted repeated score words are not exclusion proof.

Choose `apply_recall` only when every challenged block is itself target source or belongs to the same indispensable closure, the recall issue is the single proven material defect, and the resulting complete candidate has no other known material membership error. Related background, ordinary requirements, evidence forms, price or qualification material, later procedure, and useful writing context are not inclusion proof.

For candidate-edge issues, judge membership relationally. An adjacent peer can inherit an established evaluator-owned effect when the authored list, grammar, object, and lifecycle continue without an affirmative boundary; it need not repeat a score verb. Adjacency and numbering alone are still insufficient.

Choose `preserve_candidate` when neither issue is fully proven, or when both independent issues are proven so that neither permitted single change yields a complete correct result. Use `degraded` only when immutable source quality prevents reliable three-way adjudication. Cite 1 to 16 decisive source IDs and give a source-first reason.
